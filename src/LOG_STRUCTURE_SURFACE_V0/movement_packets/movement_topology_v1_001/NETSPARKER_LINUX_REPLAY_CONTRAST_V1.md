# NETSPARKER_LINUX_REPLAY_CONTRAST_V1

Status:
bounded_cross_terrain_replay_contrast

## Purpose

This note contrasts replay-visible deformation behavior between Netsparker and Linux terrains under the same constitutional reread discipline.

The goal is not terrain ranking or unification.

The goal is bounded replay comparison.

## Netsparker

Replay ecology:
- broad distributed deformation
- continual merge/split pressure
- field-wide participation
- hollow-spanning recomposition
- constitution-wide coherence

Movement style:
- braided
- circulating
- continuously recomposing

## Linux

Replay ecology:
- dominant stable carrier field
- sparse local disturbances
- delayed coherence emergence
- edge-directed redistribution
- constitution-local survivability

Movement style:
- directional
- delayed
- asymmetrically absorbed
- locally oscillatory

## Main contrast

Netsparker exposes broad coherence almost immediately but continually destabilizes and redistributes it.

Linux preserves stable carrier dominance much longer, allowing broader constitutions to selectively admit larger continuity organization.

## Important distinction

Sparse does not mean structurally empty.

Linux replay behavior suggests:
- low redistribution pressure
- localized transfer participation
- constitution-dependent continuity emergence

rather than absence of organization.

## Boundary

This contrast does not establish:
- universal terrain classes
- shared topology
- hidden field laws
- generalized replay equivalence

## Hold

The same reread machinery can expose materially different persistence and redistribution ecologies across terrains.
