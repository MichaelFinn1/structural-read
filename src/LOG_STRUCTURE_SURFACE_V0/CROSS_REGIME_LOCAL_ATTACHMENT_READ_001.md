# CROSS_REGIME_LOCAL_ATTACHMENT_READ_001

Status: observer_read_only

## Main result

Persistence regimes are not isolated bins.

Stable, middle, and residual exhibit distinct local adjacency geometries.

Residual forms may appear:

- stable-attached
- residual-clustered
- middle-bridged
- mixed-context

without implying lifecycle progression.

## Key observations

### openssh

Residual ecology is strongly stable-attached.

- residual near stable: 95.3%
- residual near residual: 25%

Frequent signatures:

- residual-stable-residual
- stable-residual-stable

Interpretation boundary:

Residual variation remains tightly embedded inside stable neighborhoods.

No claim of stabilization trajectory.

### apache_access

Residual ecology is strongly residual-clustered.

- residual near residual: 79.2%
- residual near stable: 36.9%

Frequent signatures:

- residual-residual-residual

Interpretation boundary:

Residuals frequently inhabit locally fragmented edge neighborhoods.

No anomaly claim.

### apache_error

Mixed local ecology.

Residuals exhibit both:
- stable attachment
- residual clustering

Middle circulation remains moderately stable-adjacent.

## Middle-layer distinction

Middle circulation differs across terrains.

openssh:
- middle near stable: 94.1%

apache_access:
- middle near stable: 46.5%

apache_error:
- middle near stable: 53.8%

This suggests:

middle circulation may either:
- tightly orbit stable basins
or
- circulate more independently.

## Structural correction

Residual forms are not merely isolated singletons.

Residuals may:
- cluster locally
- attach to stable bands
- bridge regimes
- persist as edge clouds

without exact-template recurrence.

## Boundary

This surface remains static/postural.

It describes local adjacency only.

It does NOT describe:
- temporal movement
- stabilization
- decay
- promotion
- causal influence

## Current hold

Local regime neighborhoods appear to form distinct ecological postures across terrains.
